# Research notes: conservative demo specimens

This note supports a small Canadian home-breeder demonstration site. The species summaries are identification and husbandry-planning references, not veterinary instructions. A keeper should confirm enclosure set-points, feeding intervals, quarantine, and health decisions with an exotics veterinarian and applicable Canadian/provincial rules. The university species accounts below document natural history; the linked care guides are secondary practical syntheses and are included only to show broad demo categories.

## Candidate species

### Ball python — *Python regius*

- **Adult context:** Common adult length is about 0.9–1.5 m (3–5 ft); females are commonly larger than males. [Animal Diversity Web species account](https://animaldiversity.org/accounts/Python_regius/)
- **Demo care note:** Model a warm/cool thermal gradient, moderate baseline humidity with a higher-humidity retreat during shed, and appropriately sized frozen-thawed rodents. A conservative planning band is warm side about 31–33 °C and 50–60% ambient humidity; treat those numbers as a secondary synthesis, not a prescription. [Merck Veterinary Manual—reptile management](https://www.merckvetmanual.com/exotic-and-laboratory-animals/reptiles/management-of-reptiles), [secondary care guide](https://reptifiles.com/ball-python-care-guide/)
- **Temperament caveat:** Captive ball pythons are often manageable, but defensive behavior and feeding response vary by individual; record handling observations rather than promising “docile.” [Animal Diversity Web](https://animaldiversity.org/accounts/Python_regius/)

### Corn snake — *Pantherophis guttatus*

- **Adult context:** Adults are generally about 0.9–1.5 m (3–5 ft). The species account describes a rodent-eating snake that is primarily terrestrial but also capable of climbing. [Animal Diversity Web species account](https://animaldiversity.org/accounts/Pantherophis_guttatus/)
- **Demo care note:** Use a warm basking area, a cooler retreat, moderate humidity, and appropriately sized frozen-thawed rodents; a conservative planning band is warm side about 29–30 °C and 40–60% humidity. These values are secondary synthesis for demo copy, not individualized advice. [Merck Veterinary Manual](https://www.merckvetmanual.com/exotic-and-laboratory-animals/reptiles/management-of-reptiles), [secondary care guide](https://reptifiles.com/corn-snake-care-guide/)
- **Temperament caveat:** Many captive corn snakes tolerate routine handling, but juveniles may be flighty and any snake can bite when stressed or mistaking movement for prey. [Animal Diversity Web](https://animaldiversity.org/accounts/Pantherophis_guttatus/)

### Western hognose — *Heterodon nasicus*

- **Adult context:** Adult size is commonly about 0.45–0.9 m (1.5–3 ft), with females typically larger; the account documents a fossorial, toad-eating specialist. [Animal Diversity Web species account](https://animaldiversity.org/accounts/Heterodon_nasicus/)
- **Demo care note:** Provide deep burrowing substrate, a warm basking zone around 32–35 °C, a cooler zone, and comparatively dry ambient humidity (roughly 30–50% planning band); offer appropriately sized rodents and monitor body condition. Ranges are secondary synthesis. [Merck Veterinary Manual](https://www.merckvetmanual.com/exotic-and-laboratory-animals/reptiles/management-of-reptiles), [secondary care guide](https://reptifiles.com/heterodon-hognose-snake-care/)
- **Temperament caveat:** Bluff displays (hissing, neck flattening, musking, and occasional playing dead) are characteristic defensive behaviors; hognoses are rear-fanged and should be handled deliberately. [Animal Diversity Web](https://animaldiversity.org/accounts/Heterodon_nasicus/), [handling reference](https://reptifiles.com/heterodon-hognose-snake-care/hognose-snake-handling-body-language/)

### Boa imperator — *Boa imperator* (formerly commonly treated as *Boa constrictor imperator*)

- **Adult context:** *B. imperator* is the currently recognized species name in the Reptile Database; adult size is variable by sex and locality, so a demo description should say “typically ~1.5–2.4 m (5–8 ft), with females often larger,” not imply a fixed maximum. [Reptile Database taxonomy](https://reptile-database.reptarium.cz/species?genus=Boa&species=imperator), [Animal Diversity Web boa account](https://animaldiversity.org/accounts/Boa_constrictor/)
- **Demo care note:** Plan a secure, well-ventilated enclosure with a warm basking zone around 31–33 °C, cooler retreat, and moderate-to-high humidity (roughly 60–70% planning band); feed appropriately sized rodents on an age- and condition-appropriate schedule. Values are secondary synthesis. [Merck Veterinary Manual](https://www.merckvetmanual.com/exotic-and-laboratory-animals/reptiles/management-of-reptiles), [secondary care guide](https://reptifiles.com/boa-constrictor-care/)
- **Temperament caveat:** A large constrictor requires handling and enclosure procedures that account for adult strength; individual temperament is not guaranteed by species or morph. [Animal Diversity Web](https://animaldiversity.org/accounts/Boa_constrictor/)

### Kenyan sand boa — *Eryx colubrinus*

- **Adult context:** Adults are usually about 0.45–0.75 m (1.5–2.5 ft), with females substantially heavier/larger; the species account describes a predominantly fossorial ambush predator. [Animal Diversity Web species account](https://animaldiversity.org/accounts/Eryx_colubrinus/)
- **Demo care note:** Emphasize deep, dry, diggable substrate; provide a warm zone around 31–33 °C, a cooler retreat, lower ambient humidity (roughly 30–50%), and appropriately sized rodents. Values are secondary synthesis and should not be used as a medical protocol. [Merck Veterinary Manual](https://www.merckvetmanual.com/exotic-and-laboratory-animals/reptiles/management-of-reptiles), [secondary care guide](https://reptifiles.com/kenyan-sand-boa-care/)
- **Temperament caveat:** Sand boas spend much of their time buried and may be startled when uncovered; surface visibility is not a reliable welfare or activity measure. [Animal Diversity Web](https://animaldiversity.org/accounts/Eryx_colubrinus/)

### California kingsnake — *Lampropeltis californiae*

- **Adult context:** Adults are commonly around 0.9–1.2 m (3–4 ft), with some larger; the university account describes a broadly opportunistic diet that includes other snakes. [Animal Diversity Web species account](https://animaldiversity.org/accounts/Lampropeltis_californiae/)
- **Demo care note:** Use a warm basking zone around 29–32 °C, a cooler retreat, moderate-to-low humidity (roughly 30–50% planning band), and appropriately sized frozen-thawed rodents. Because a stable first-party kingsnake care sheet was not found, present these as conservative planning synthesis only and verify locally. [Merck Veterinary Manual](https://www.merckvetmanual.com/exotic-and-laboratory-animals/reptiles/management-of-reptiles), [Animal Diversity Web](https://animaldiversity.org/accounts/Lampropeltis_californiae/)
- **Temperament caveat:** Kingsnakes can be food-motivated and may show defensive feeding behavior; never co-house them, because the species is known to eat other snakes. [Animal Diversity Web](https://animaldiversity.org/accounts/Lampropeltis_californiae/)

## Genetics wording

Use “morph” only as a neutral label for a captive color/pattern phenotype. Store breeder-supplied gene names and “ het ” (heterozygous) claims as provenance-bearing fields; do not infer inheritance, genotype, or health from appearance. The Reptile Database is a taxonomic authority, not a morph-genetics registry, so this note intentionally avoids asserting inheritance modes without a verifiable breeder record. [Reptile Database](https://reptile-database.reptarium.cz/)

Two conservative trait-chip examples do have direct peer-reviewed support:

| Species/morph | Supported demo label | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ball python Piebald | `recessive` | The study identifies the genetic basis of the “recessive piebald phenotype” and associates it with a nonsense mutation in *tfec*. [Current Biology](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cub.2023.01.004) |
| Corn snake Amelanistic | `recessive` | Breeding crosses demonstrated a single-locus recessive mutation associated with an insertion in *OCA2*. [Scientific Reports](https://doi.org/10.1038/srep17118) |

Do not assign inheritance chips to Pastel, Banana, Clown, Tessera, Palmetto, Arctic, Axanthic, Anerythristic, or Hypomelanistic solely from the morph name in demo data. Their commonly used hobby inheritance labels were not verified here from a primary genetics source; keep them as explicitly provenance-labeled breeder or registry text instead. “Codominant” is also not interchangeable with “incomplete dominant”: the US National Human Genome Research Institute defines [codominance](https://www.genome.gov/genetics-glossary/Codominance) as both allele traits being expressed, while the [OpenStax genetics text](https://openstax.org/books/biology-2e/pages/12-3-laws-of-inheritance) distinguishes that outcome from incomplete dominance's intermediate phenotype. Use the precise term supplied by a cited genetics source rather than mapping one to the other for convenience.

## Demo-record coverage and fiction boundary

Every specimen name, slug, description, price in CAD, availability/featured flags, image key, sex, hatch date, weight in grams, status, genetics text, and individualized personality/feeding/shed/temperature/humidity note used by the app must be explicitly labeled **fictional demo data**. They are not real animals, inventory, provenance, or medical advice.

To exercise the application schema without implying real stock, create clearly marked fictional records that collectively cover:

| Field/status | Coverage suggestion |
| --- | --- |
| `slug`, `name`, `species`, `description` | One row per species above; include scientific name in the description. |
| `priceInCents` (CAD), `available`, `featured`, `primaryImageKey` | Use invented values and placeholder image keys; include both true/false flags. |
| `sex`, `hatchDate`, `weightGrams` | Invent values and dates; do not present them as measured animals. |
| `personality`, `feedingNotes`, `diet`, `shedFrequency`, `temperature`, `humidity` | Phrase as generic species-level demo copy and link back to the references above. |
| `complementaryGenetics` | Use “normal/wild-type” or a provenance-labeled morph placeholder; avoid unsupported inheritance claims. |
| `status` | Include fictional rows in each supported state: `available`, `reserved`, and `sold`. |
